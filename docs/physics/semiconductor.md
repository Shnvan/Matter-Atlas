# Semiconductor foundations

Pure silicon is already a semiconductor. Bonding and available electron energy states yield a gap between occupied valence states and conduction states; thermal excitation can create mobile electron/hole carriers. Doping changes carrier concentration and makes behavior controllable; it does not create silicon's semiconductor identity. [SRC-018](../../SOURCES.md)

## Simplified lattice and dopants

Use a covalent-bond/lattice diagram as a simplified model, not a photograph or full quantum structure. Silicon valence bonding can be illustrated with phosphorus donor and boron acceptor examples. N-type has electrons as majority carriers; p-type has holes as majority carriers. The bulk is approximately electrically neutral, including ionized dopants; neither is simply a charged block. A hole is an effective positive carrier associated with missing occupation in valence states, not a literal empty particle floating through space. [SRC-018](../../SOURCES.md)

## Junction and field

Joining p/n regions leads to carrier redistribution and a depleted region with internal electric field. Applied bias changes the barrier/carrier flow. Explain forward/reverse bias before the diode analogy. MOS gates similarly control carriers with an electric field across dielectric, though the structures and operating conditions differ. [SRC-019, SRC-031](../../SOURCES.md)
